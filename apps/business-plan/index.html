    Newcastle NSW

    How a Newcastle songwriter runs a small creative brand. A simple plan for Karen's Coffeebreak brand: what it sells, who buys it, how it gets found, and how to grow song royalties over the next 90 days.

    This plan is free to read. When a Coffeebreak page sells something, checkout is a shopping cart plus Pay by PayID (Teachers Mutual Bank): darkokaz@gmx.com.

    1. Vision

    Coffeebreak is a pink, friendly creator brand from Newcastle NSW. Karen writes and releases songs as Coffeebreak, and sells apps, games, merch and home fixtures that carry the Coffeebreak name. The goal is steady income from many small sales, with no large upfront ad spend, while the music builds listeners who then shop on the site.

    3. Target customers

    • Listeners who already follow the songs and want a small digital pack or a piece of merch.
    • People buying a printable character pack for personal use.
    • Buyers in Australia who need a large sink or a bath delivered, and who will hire a licensed plumber to install it.
    • Collectors of the character plush and flash prints.

    The brand speaks to people who like the pink Coffeebreak look. It does not sort buyers by private life.

    4. Channels

    • The store is this website: songwriterkaz.github.io/coffeebreak.
    • The A-Z directory on the site points at each live page.
    • Payment is Pay by PayID (Teachers Mutual Bank): darkokaz@gmx.com. Orders are emailed to the same address.
    • Music stays on the platforms already linked from Coffeebreak pages.
    • YouTube Coffeebreak TV Series is the public face of the characters.

    5. Marketing plan

    • Each product has one page with the logo, a plain description, the price and a cart.
    • Songs mention the site. The site mentions the songs.
    • Reuse artwork already on the site. Do not add personal phone photos.
    • Keep the directory in step with the pages, so a closed offer is not still advertised.
    • Spend time on pages that can take a PayID, rather than on ads that need cash up front.

    6. Operations

    • A buyer adds the item to the cart, pays the PayID, and puts the cart reference on the transfer.
    • A note that says "I have paid" is not proof. Karen checks the bank, then sends digital access or places the heavy-goods order.
    • Digital files stay on the site for the buyer to print. Personal use. The buyer may not copy the files to make their own version for sale.
    • Heavy fixtures are bought in and shipped after payment. The public page does not name the company the goods come from.
    • Sinks and baths are sold as goods. Installation is by a licensed plumber.
    • Remote freight is confirmed by email before the buyer pays, when the delivery allowance may not cover it.

    7. Financial goals

    • Many small sales, not one large contract.
    • Digital packs at US$10.
    • Heavy physical items priced above US$10 so the sale covers the cost of the goods, a delivery allowance and profit. If a price cannot do that, the item is not listed.
    • The aim is a month where shop sales and song royalties together cover ordinary running costs, then leave a surplus. This plan does not invent a dollar forecast.

    8. Growing song royalties

    • Keep releasing songs as Coffeebreak on the platforms already in use.
    • Put the song link on the hub when a release is live.
    • Where a page and a song share a theme, mention both. Do not promise a royalty rate. The platforms report the royalties.
    • Over 90 days the measure is releases shipped and pages that point at them, not a guessed stream count.

    9. Risks

    • PayID is manual. Shipping or unlocking before the transfer lands is a loss.
    • Freight on a bath or a large sink can exceed the allowance.
    • A price set too low sells at a loss. Heavy items stay above cost.
    • Brand names on resale fixtures belong to their owners. Coffeebreak does not claim those brands.
    • A broken cart script stops a sale even when the rest of the page looks fine. Check the cart after a change.
    • A disclaimer cannot remove rights under the Australian Consumer Law.

    10. 90-day action plan

    Tick an item to keep it on this device.
