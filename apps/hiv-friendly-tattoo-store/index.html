Everyone is welcome here

Coffeebreak Tattoo is an HIV friendly, stigma free space. Everyone is welcome, including people living with HIV.

  • No one is ever refused a tattoo or charged extra because of their HIV status.
  • You never have to tell us your HIV status. Disclosure is never required.
  • If you choose to share anything about your health, it stays confidential and is only used to look after you.
  • The same high standard of infection control is used for every single client, every single time.

We want every client to feel safe, respected and proud of their art.

Infection control for every client

We use standard precautions (also called universal precautions) for everyone. That means we treat every client the same way, so nobody needs to disclose anything for us to keep everyone safe.

  • Sterile single use needles and cartridges, opened in front of you
  • Single use ink caps, with ink never returned to the bottle
  • New single use gloves for each client, changed whenever needed
  • Barrier film and sleeves on machines, cords, bottles and touch points, replaced for every client
  • Sterile or single use equipment, with any reusable equipment cleaned and sterilised in an autoclave
  • Sharps placed straight into an approved sharps container
  • Work surfaces cleaned and disinfected between clients, and hand washing before and after every client

This is general information about how we work. It is not medical advice and is not a certification. For the rules that apply to tattooing in NSW, see the NSW Health skin penetration guidelines: health.nsw.gov.au/environment/skinpenetration.

Aftercare and care tips

Aftercare for clients

First 24 hours

  • Leave the wrap or film on for the time your artist tells you.
  • Wash your hands before touching the tattoo. Wash it gently with lukewarm water and a mild unscented soap, then pat dry with clean paper towel.
  • Some weeping of plasma, ink and a little blood is normal.

Week 1

  • Wash gently two to three times a day and apply a very thin layer of the aftercare product your artist recommends.
  • Do not soak it: no baths, swimming pools, spas, the ocean or saunas.
  • Wear loose, clean clothing over it and use clean bedding.
  • Do not scratch, pick or peel. Mild redness, swelling and tenderness are common at first.

Weeks 2 to 4

  • Flaking and itching are normal. Let flakes fall off on their own.
  • Keep moisturising lightly. Keep avoiding soaking until the skin has fully closed.

Long term and sun care

  • Keep it out of direct sun while healing. Once healed, use broad spectrum SPF 50+ sunscreen and cover up to stop fading.
  • Keep skin moisturised. Book a touch up with your artist if needed.

Signs of infection and when to see a GP

  • See a GP promptly if you notice redness or swelling that spreads or gets worse after the first few days, increasing pain or heat, pus or a bad smell, red streaks, fever or chills, or a rash or raised itchy lumps that may be an allergic reaction.
  • Call 000 in an emergency. For advice you can call healthdirect on 1800 022 222.

Before you get tattooed

  • Eat a proper meal beforehand and drink plenty of water.
  • Get a good night's sleep. Do not drink alcohol or use drugs before your appointment.
  • Bring photo ID. You must be 18 or over to be tattooed in NSW.
  • Tell your artist about medical conditions and medicines, including diabetes, bleeding or clotting disorders, blood thinners, heart conditions, epilepsy, immune conditions, skin conditions, allergies (including latex), pregnancy or breastfeeding.
  • Do not get tattooed over broken, sunburnt or irritated skin. Wear comfortable clothing that gives access to the area.

Artist safety and hygiene

  • Use sterile single use needles and cartridges, opened in front of the client. Use single use ink caps and never return unused ink to the bottle.
  • Wash and dry hands before and after each client and whenever gloves are changed. Wear new single use gloves for each client and change them if torn or contaminated.
  • Cover machines, cords, bottles and touch points with barrier film or sleeves and replace them for every client.
  • Put used needles and sharps straight into an approved sharps container. Dispose of sharps and clinical waste as required by your council and NSW Health.
  • Clean and disinfect work surfaces, chairs and equipment between clients with a suitable hospital grade disinfectant, and keep the workstation clutter free.
  • Treat all blood and body fluids as potentially infectious. Use standard precautions to prevent the spread of bloodborne viruses such as hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV. Artists should consider hepatitis B vaccination and know what to do after a needlestick injury.
  • Keep records as required and follow the infection control guidelines that apply to your premises.
