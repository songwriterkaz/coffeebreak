Catch a Catfish

Spot fake profiles before they hook you

Level 1

Cast your line and see what bites. Each catch is an invented profile. Decide if it is a real friend or a catfish.

Please read first. This is a checklist, not a detective. It cannot unmask anyone and it does not look anyone up. Your answers stay on this page, nothing is uploaded and nothing is saved.
  • Do not confront, threaten or shame the person.
  • Do not send money, gift cards or crypto, and do not share personal details, ID or bank information.
  • Stop contact, keep screenshots, then report.
  • Do not post accusations. If you are wrong, a real person can be hurt.

1Check the photos

Open a reverse image search in a new tab, then upload or paste one of their profile photos. Stock photos and stolen photos often show up under other names.

Google ImagesTap the camera icon to search by image Google LensSearch with a photo or screenshot TinEyeReverse image search, finds where else a photo appears

2Check the account

3Check the chat

4Try a video call test

A real person is usually happy to say hello on a short live video call. Suggest one on the same app you already use, and keep your phone number and other accounts private.

  • Ask for a quick live video call, a few minutes is enough.
  • During the call, ask them to do something simple and unscripted, like show their hand next to their face or read out today's date.
  • Watch for a pre-recorded look, a frozen picture, a face that does not match the photos, or a lot of excuses.
  • A call that goes well does not prove everything is fine. Keep other checks going.

6Report in Australia

Reporting helps stop the person from hurting others. If you are in immediate danger, call 000. If you sent money, call your bank first, using the number on your card.
