3D print

Safety notice for live printer control. A 3D printer has a hot nozzle (200 °C+), a heated bed and moving parts. Only start a print when you are at the printer, the bed is clear and you will watch it. Never leave a printer unattended. Use Emergency stop if anything looks wrong. Coffeebreak House Maker sends commands exactly as shown; you are responsible for your printer's settings and safety.

1. Export for your slicer (recommended)

STL and 3MF files are watertight, in millimetres, Z up, ready for PrusaSlicer, Cura, Bambu Studio or OrcaSlicer. Open in slicer: download the file, then drag it into your slicer (or double-click the 3MF). This is the most reliable way to print.

2. Built-in basic slicer (experimental)

Honest note: the built-in slicer is basic. It makes perimeters and straight-line infill with no supports, no bridging tuning and no printer profiles. Ceilings and window tops print as short bridges, which most printers handle at 1:200 and smaller, but large rooms may sag. For best results use the STL in a real slicer, or print one storey at a time.

3. Connect a printer

USB (Marlin) via Web Serial

Works in desktop Chrome or Edge with the printer plugged in by USB. Not available on iPhone, iPad or Firefox.

Not connected

Network: OctoPrint or Moonraker/Klipper

Browsers block a secure https page from talking to a printer on plain http. If the test fails, download the full app and open it from your computer, give your printer an https address, or allow this site in OctoPrint (Settings → API → Allow Cross Origin) or Moonraker's cors_domains. You can always download the G-code and upload it in your printer's own web page.
